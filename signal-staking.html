Signal vs staking

Separating whether a selection beat its price from how much was risked on it.

The distinction the leaderboard was getting wrong

ROI is stake times outcome, summed. It therefore mixes two things a reader needs separately:

Signal
Did the selection beat the price? Measured price-free as hit rate − mean break-even rate, where break-even is 1 / decimal price. No stake enters this number, so no staking pattern can move it.
Staking
How much was risked on each selection. Its entire effect on ROI is the covariance between stake and outcome. A rule that happens to stake more on winners manufactures a positive ROI out of a negative signal.

Signal, price-free

Hit rate is wins over decided tickets. Break-even rate is the average 1/decimal price the selections were bought at. A positive signal means the strategy won more often than its own average price required — regardless of stake.

These values describe the archived prices, not a bet. The prices are undated community values with no book, no timestamp and no limit (see prices.html). They are printed here so the decomposition can be audited, and they are not promoted to a result. The staking-form test — the part that needs no authenticated price

The same selection list is re-scored under three alternative staking rules. Both adaptive rules use only past outcomes, so neither is look-ahead.

  • FLAT — 1 unit every ticket. The neutral baseline.
  • MARTINGALE — stake doubles after a loss, resets on a win, capped at 8 units.
  • RECENCY — stake scaled by the trailing-10 hit rate, bounded to 0.25–2.0 units.

2 strategies change the sign of their return under a staking rule alone: ML_EDGE_5, ROAD_FAV_ATS. For those, the published ROI is partly a stake artefact and the flat number must be the only one quoted.

What this changes on the leaderboard

  1. Signal and staking are now two columns, never one. A strategy can no longer be ranked on ROI without its price-free signal being visible on the same row.
  2. A sign flip blocks promotion outright. The demotion gate (demotion.html) treats a staking-induced sign change as trigger D6 and demotes the strategy regardless of its ROI.
  3. Stake size may not be optimised before the signal is established. Optimising a stake rule against a backtest is itself a search over the sample; the eight validation gates already require the complete trial count to be registered.
