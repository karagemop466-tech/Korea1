Replacement depth

Depth of the hole, not the fact of it: official injuries joined to official club depth charts.

What this page is. Requirement 6 of the 2026-11-14 brief: track the depth of the replacement, not the fact of a replacement. A club losing its first string counts for more than a club losing its fourth string, and a club with no listed backup behind a starter is in a different position from one with three. This page joins the official injury snapshot to the official club depth charts and prints the depth of every hole.

1 · The measure, and what it refuses to claim

depth_impact = Σ 1/rank over the club's unavailable players, so a first-string loss is 1.00, a second-string loss 0.50, a third 0.33 and a fourth 0.25 — losing a starter counts four times losing a fourth-string player, and two second-string losses (1.00) equal one first-string loss. Net impact = offence − defence, so a depleted defence (which helps the opponent's offence) reads negative. That sign convention is a published convention, not a model output.

What the measure does not do:

  • It does not claim a listed backup plays. A club depth chart carries no publication time and no game-time confirmation, so the depth is the club's published order.
  • It does not assign a depth to a player the chart does not list. Those are counted as unmatched and reported — 72 today — rather than being treated as a first-string loss.
  • It does not assess a club with no parsed chart. 1 club(s) have none; their rows read NO_CHART and are excluded from the statistics rather than defaulted.

5 · Why this is not yet a signal

Depth is a condition, not a forecast. Turning it into a signal needs two things this build does not have: (a) a confirmed game-day inactive list per club — the official pregame report is published 90 minutes before kickoff and is not archived here; and (b) a measured relationship between depth loss and scoring, estimated out of sample. Both are proposed as DEP-01 and DEP-02 on the suggestions page, and neither is claimed as a result.
