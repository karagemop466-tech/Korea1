Vig-free consensus vs originated fair line

How much of our gap to the market is information and how much is the bookmaker's fee — posted break-even, vig-free consensus and margin cost as separate columns, per season and market, with n on every row.

Evidence boundary. The prices in this view are the pinned community archive of the league's game-level data — an open mirror, not an authenticated operator quote. That is why gate G1 fails for every candidate and why no number on this page is a ranking, a stake or an expected value. The model column is our own output, stored as a probability.

What the decomposition is

For every game where the archive prices both sides of a market, three probabilities can be computed without inventing anything:

  1. Posted break-even — the probability the offered price demands, vig included. At −110 both sides this is 0.5238, not 0.5000.
  2. Vig-free consensus — the same two prices with the overround removed proportionally (pa / (pa + pb)). This is the market's opinion, not our opinion of it.
  3. Margin cost — the difference of those two, in probability points. It is the part of any apparent edge that is the bookmaker's fee.

Our model's probability is then split against them: gross gap = model − posted break-even and divergence = model − vig-free consensus, which obey gross gap = divergence − margin cost. That identity is asserted for all 16,206 rows at build time; the build fails rather than publishes a decomposition whose arithmetic does not close.

Two modelling choices are stated rather than hidden. A one-pair price cannot reveal the book's true line, so the vig-free result is published as a probability and never converted back into points. And the market quotes a two-way price while our model publishes three outcomes (win / push / lose), so our probability is conditioned on a decisive game; the push mass removed is printed per row so the conditioning is visible.

Integrity disclosure. One published quantity here can be negative, and it is reported rather than repaired: rows below parity counts games where the archive's two prices imply together less than 100% — an inconsistent pair in the mirror, not an opportunity. The minimum overround on each row shows how far below parity it goes; the affected games stay in the store, and their margin cost is published as the negative number it is.

3 · What this view does and does not decide

  • It does decide whether a divergence is larger than the vig. If the margin cost exceeds the divergence, there is no bet to size whatever else is true — which is the sequencing the request asks for ("before any bet structure is considered").
  • It does not measure whether our divergence is right. That is a settlement question, answered per game on the origination page and gated by the eight gates; a divergence that is consistently wrong is still a divergence here, and the register says so.
  • It does not price a "no-vig line" in points. The archive posts one line with two side prices, which identifies a probability, not a line; publishing a converted points number would be an assumption wearing a decimal place.

Method references kept on the same footing as the rest of the site: the proportional de-vig is the same novig_probs() helper the model already uses for the moneyline (pipeline/common.py), applied here to the spread and total prices for the first time.
