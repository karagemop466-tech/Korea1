Signal families and why they might matter

  • Model disagreement: a trade only has positive expected value if the model is better calibrated than the available price, not merely different from it.
  • Situational angles: rest, weather and recent form are hypotheses. Their availability timestamps and multiple-testing burden must be controlled.
  • Parlays: multiplying standalone prices does not establish an executable correlated ticket. Distinct games can share latent slate effects.
  • Player props: opportunity, role and game-script dependence matter, but no verified price history means no validated prop strategy.
  • Closing-line value: a useful prospective diagnostic only with genuine entry timestamps and comparable bookmaker closing quotes; it is not a guarantee of profit.

Signal and staking, separated

Download both scorecards →

A leaderboard sorted on money ranks luck. So the two questions are measured apart and published apart. The signal scorecard asks whether the rule is right: how often its direction was correct, against the rate it had to reach to pay for itself at the price each bet actually carried. The staking scorecard asks whether the money worked: the realised return, its spread and its drawdown. The leaderboard is ranked on the signal; the money columns are shown beside it and never order it.

The rule that makes the separation bite: a positive return may never promote a strategy whose signal is not positive. Such a strategy is labelled a lucky staking run and is capped at paper, whatever its position on the money table.
