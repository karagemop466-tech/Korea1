Leaderboard design

A competition that pays for measured skill, not for one lucky season.

Built 2026-11-01 13:50 UTC · data window 2011–2021 · every value computed by a script in scripts/ · verification 9/9 checks

Why the score is not profit

A leaderboard that ranks on season profit is a lottery. The evidence, all measured in this project:

  • A field of 1,000 bettors with zero skill produces a median winner at 18.7% ROI after one 267-bet season.
  • A bettor who genuinely has a 2% edge finishes first 0.32% of the time when ranked by ROI — and 78.9% of the time when ranked by closing-line value.
  • Proving a 2% edge by win/loss alone needs 17,796 bets — about 67 NFL seasons.

So the score is Luck-adjusted skill score (LASS):

LASS = lam * (0.5*tanh(C/2) + 0.3*tanh(R/2) + 0.2*(2*DSR - 1)), lam = n/(n+400)

Weights: CLV 0.5, return 0.3, selection-bias correction 0.2. Shrinkage n₀ = 400 bets, so a small sample is pulled toward zero and an early-season fluke cannot lead the table. Hard ceiling: a strategy that fails G8 (Deflated Sharpe Ratio < 0.95) cannot rank above Paper, whatever its LASS.

Nobody reaches Gold or Silver. That is the intended behaviour of the gate set, not a bug: a competition that produced a Gold winner from 18 rules on this data would be awarding luck.

Anti-gaming rules

  • Bet timestamps and line snapshots are mandatory — without them CLV is unfalsifiable.
  • Editing a published rule resets it to Paper and consumes one trial from the field's multiple-testing budget.
  • No cherry-picked subsamples: subsets must be reported with the whole history, and the filter counts as a trial.
  • Parlays are scored on correlation-adjusted expected value, checked against the measured correlation table.
  • Flat staking by default; the score uses the shrunk unit series so leverage cannot manufacture a ranking.
  • Arbitrage and bonus abuse are excluded — they are not signals about the game.
  • Minimum bet counts per tier are enforced, which is the direct application of the power arithmetic.
