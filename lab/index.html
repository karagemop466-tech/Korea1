Headline findings

  1. Our own line is good, and still worse than the market. Walk-forward Elo + pace model: spread MAE 10.47 pts vs 10.15 for the closing line; straight-up 66.4% vs 67.3%. detail →
  2. No pre-specified strategy survived all eight validation gates. Gate counts: G1 16/18, G2 0/18, G3 2/18, G8 0/18. Two results are worth watching, none is fundable. detail →
  3. Model-edge betting is reliably unprofitable, not randomly unprofitable. S01 ROI -10.92% at p < 0.001 after false-discovery correction. The market beats the model in the exact spots where they disagree. detail →
  4. The QB/WR1 stack is the one genuinely mispriced combination available. Measured ρ = 0.441 (within-player). The empirical joint is 35.6% versus 25.5% if the legs were independent — a 39.7% gap, i.e. a fair price near 2.81 rather than 3.92. detail →
  5. WR1 and WR2 are mildly positively correlated — the intuitive 'hedge' is backwards. Within-player ρ = 0.182 for receptions; game environment dominates target competition. detail →
  6. A season-P&L leaderboard cannot find skill; a CLV leaderboard can. A bettor with a true 2% edge finishes first 0.32% of the time on ROI and 78.9% on closing-line value. detail →
  7. The house edge on combinations is roughly three times the straight-bet edge. Nevada Gaming Control Board, November 2025: Sports Parlay Cards 19.94% win over twelve months vs Sports Pool 6.74% (official regulator data). detail →

Method in one paragraph

The market price is the benchmark, so the project starts by measuring exactly what it costs to bet (3.79% overround on closing moneylines, 6–7% realised hold in Nevada). It then builds an independent line engine from results only, tests 18 rules that a bettor would plausibly use, and refuses to accept any of them without clearing sample-size, false-discovery, out-of-sample, robustness, closing-line-value, risk, availability and selection-bias gates. Correlations are measured from play-by-play rather than assumed, and the leaderboard is scored on luck-adjusted, CLV-weighted skill because the simulation shows a raw-profit board is a coin flip.

Research artifact. No betting advice, no live wagering, no guarantee of profit. Values that could not be confirmed against a source are marked unverified and listed on the Flagged for review page.
