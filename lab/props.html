What is measured here, and what is not. These correlations are computed from 543 regular-season games of play-by-play (2021–2022), 1,086 team-games. They are real. What is not available is a licence-clean history of player-prop prices, so this page makes no claim about prop profitability — only about how legs co-move and therefore what a parlay should cost. 'Overpayment' is how much more a book pays than the combination is worth. Negative values mean an independent price would underpay the bettor's true chance (the anti-correlated QB/RB case).

The three findings worth acting on

  1. QB × WR1 is the mispricing to beat: ρ = 0.441, a 39.7% gap between the empirical joint and independence, replicated in both seasons.
  2. WR1 × WR2 is not a hedge: ρ = 0.182 for receptions — positive. Pricing it as negatively correlated mis-prices it in the wrong direction.
  3. QB × RB1 is the only clearly negative pair (ρ = -0.135): game script moves passing and rushing volume in opposite directions, so that combination is worth less than an independent price implies.
