Status: player-level data is real, prop prices are not

Corrected in this build. An earlier version of this page said player-level data could not be retrieved from any reachable source. That was wrong: nflverse play-by-play is mirrored as gzip blobs inside a public GitHub repository and is reachable through the GitHub git-blob API, which this environment can call. Four seasons (2019–2022) are now downloaded, SHA-256 hashed and used below.

What is still missing is prop prices. No reachable source carries a player-prop price, so no prop ROI can be computed. Every combo therefore fails gate G6 (economic significance) and nothing on this page is awarded. 1 · The data, and proof the definitions are right

Box scores are rebuilt from the play-by-play: passing yards from passing_yards on the passer, rushing yards from rushing_yards on the rusher, receiving yards and receptions from receiving_yards and complete_pass on the receiver. 2-point conversions are excluded. 2 · Originated prop lines, out of sample

For every player-week the model uses only that player's earlier games: an exponentially weighted mean (120-day half-life) shrunk toward a prior-season league mean, with a shifted-lognormal predictive distribution for yardage and a negative binomial for receptions. The published line is the half-integer nearest the predictive median, so it is a genuine 50/50 line rather than a line at the mean — which matters, because yardage is right-skewed and the mean sits above the median.

The AUC column is the probability that a randomly chosen “over” is ranked above a randomly chosen “under”. 0.5 is a coin flip. The calibration slope is the regression of the realised outcome on the model's log-odds: 1.0 is perfect calibration, 0 is no information. The slope is far below 1 on every market, which means the probabilities are under-confident — they order outcomes correctly but are compressed toward 50%. That is a fixable modelling defect, not an edge, and it is reported rather than tuned away.

3 · How correlated the legs actually are

Every pair below is measured on the same games, at each leg's originated 50/50 line. φ is the correlation of the two over/under indicators; lift is the ratio of the joint hit rate to the product of the two marginal rates, i.e. exactly the factor a parlay calculator gets wrong when it multiplies. Within-player r removes each player's own level, so a good player matched with a bad one cannot create a fake relationship.

“Odds if multiplied” is what a book returns when it prices each leg independently and multiplies. “Fair odds (joint)” is what the measured joint frequency implies. The gap is the structural mispricing. It is a reason to prefer correlated legs if the book multiplies, and it is exactly what a book's same-game pricing engine is supposed to remove — which is why the price, not the correlation, is the thing that has to be verified before any of this becomes a bet.

G6 asks whether the signal survives the cost of the market. With no prop price feed it cannot be answered, so it is scored as a fail rather than assumed. Nothing is awarded, and the prize pool for prop combos stays unawarded — the same rule the main leaderboard applies.

6 · What this page does not tell you

  • No ROI. Without prop prices there is no bet, no stake and no return. Any number presented as a prop ROI in this repository would be invented.
  • Appearance selection. A player only enters the player-game table when the play-by-play records an event for them, so a game in which a receiver ran routes but was never targeted is invisible. Averages are therefore conditional on involvement, which biases them upward. It is the main reason the marginal over-rates are not exactly 50%.
  • Our line is not a book's line. A book's prop line reflects injury status, snap share, coverage matchup and sharp money. Ours uses none of those. The correlation structure is robust to that gap; the level of any individual line is not.
  • Touchdown markets are excluded. They are near-Poisson with a large zero mass and need a much larger sample to price honestly than four seasons provide.
  • Roles are inferred from prior usage, not from a roster feed: QB = most prior pass attempts, WR1/WR2 = most prior targets, RB1 = most prior carries. That is knowable before kickoff, which is the property that matters.
