Correlation & parlay lab

Measured correlation values for single bets, same-game parlays and cross-game parlays — with the vig structure made explicit.

The maths used everywhere on this page

phi        = (P(A∩B) − P(A)P(B)) / sqrt(P(A)(1−P(A))P(B)(1−P(B)))
lift       = P(A∩B) / (P(A)·P(B))              # 1.00 = independent
fair dec.  = 1 / P(A∩B)
EV per 1u  = P(A∩B)·(book_decimal − 1) − (1 − P(A∩B))

P(A), P(B) and P(A∩B) are measured on 6,880 settled games with two-sided prices; they are never assumed.

What the correlations mean

  • Spread × moneyline is the only strongly correlated pair (phi ≈ 0.67): backing a team's spread and its moneyline is effectively one bet. Both count as one unit of risk.
  • Spread × total is close to independent (phi ≈ 0.013), which is why "side and total" parlays behave like two separate bets.
  • Moneyline × total is also near zero (phi ≈ 0.001).

Cross-game legs are where books genuinely pay the product of the decimals, so this table is the real 'parlay tax'. Legs are drawn independently across games.

Reading the tax

Two legs cost about +7.9% of stake, three cost +14.4% and four cost +15.4%. Because each leg carries its own hold, stacking legs multiplies the take: the measured lift stays at ~1.0 (games are independent), so there is no correlation "discount" to recover the cost. Any parlay strategy therefore has to beat a bigger baseline than a single bet.
