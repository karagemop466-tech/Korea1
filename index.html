Executive summary

Build a pricing process, not a picks list.

Use football data to estimate distributions; use recorded market prices to decide whether any estimate is actionable. Do not confuse a successful outcome with proof of skill.

01
Single bets

Start with game-level distributions

Estimate point margin and total points from opponent-adjusted team performance. Price spreads, moneylines and totals from the distribution only after out-of-sample calibration and price comparison.

Strategy design →
02
Player props

Model opportunity before efficiency

Separate playing time and usage (attempts, carries, targets) from per-opportunity production. Official NFL stats, Next Gen Stats and the latest injury/inactive information are candidate inputs—not proof of an edge.

Prop design →
03
Parlays & combos

Price the joint event

Do not multiply leg probabilities unless independence is supported. Estimate the joint distribution and its uncertainty; publish no combination price until the correlations and settlement terms are verified.

The operating rule

Luck cannot be removed. It can be measured.

Football outcomes remain uncertain. The defensible goal is to separate predictive skill from noise with probability scores, temporal holdouts, confidence intervals and a pre-registered decision rule—not to promise certainty.

Current conclusion: The research blueprint is ready; no empirical value was filled without an aligned, permission-appropriate dataset. The correlation table, leaderboard metrics, line prices and reward pool remain blank or unpopulated and are called out for review.
