Research category · 03

Parlay math begins with dependence.

Correlation is not a decorative adjustment. It changes the probability of the joint ticket. This register names the relationships that must be measured and leaves every unsupported result blank.

!
No numerical correlation values have been calculated

The repository contains no paired player-by-game observations or historical prop lines. NFL GSIS documents official gamebook and play-by-play outputs; NIST defines correlation and its interpretation. Without an authorized, aligned sample, entering a value, sign or sample size would be an inference and is deliberately not done.

Measurement rules

What will be computed

Choose the unit and condition before the calculation. Report both observed outcome dependence and conditional residual dependence; do not treat a league-wide pooled number as universal across players, roles, lines or game states.

Continuous outcomes · Pearson correlation

ρ(X,Y) = Cov(X,Y) / (σX · σY)

NIST describes Pearson correlation as a measure of strength of a usually linear relationship, bounded from −1 to +1. It is descriptive—not a causal effect or a joint betting probability. NIST glossary ↗

Binary prop outcomes · phi correlation

φ(A,B) = [P(A∩B) − P(A)P(B)] / √[P(A)(1−P(A))P(B)(1−P(B))]

For two over/under indicators, estimate the joint event directly and report marginal probabilities and the observed sample alongside the coefficient. Correlation reference ↗

Parlay probability: for legs A and B, use P(A∩B) from the observed or modeled joint distribution. The product P(A) × P(B) is only justified if independence is supported for that defined population and context. Use game-level clustered uncertainty intervals so multiple players from the same game are not treated as independent games. See the NIST bootstrap guidance ↗.
