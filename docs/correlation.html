Why correlation is the whole game in same-game parlays

Two 50/50 legs in the same game are almost never independent. If their correlation is ρ, the fair joint probability is exactly P = 1/4 + arcsin(ρ)/(2π) — a closed form this project verifies against a 400,000-draw Monte Carlo. The table below turns that formula into prices.

At ρ = 0.44 — the measured QB/WR1 value — a fair two-leg parlay pays 3.10, not 4.00. A book that multiplied independent prices would be paying 29% over the odds. That is why sportsbooks restrict same-game parlays, and why the only edge left is in legs the book under-correlates.

φ is the correlation of the two binary outcomes. A pair with φ ≈ 0 is safe to multiply; everything else is not.

The two extremes are instructive. Home moneyline × home spread has φ = 0.635: combining them is close to betting the same thing twice, and an independent price would overpay by 58.4%. Over × home spread has φ = 0.024 — genuinely near-independent, which is why that combination is the one bettors can price by multiplication without being robbed.
