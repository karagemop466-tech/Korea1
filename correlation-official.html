Correlations, officially sourced

Dependence estimates from officially sourced values only, with n and a week-block interval on every row.

What this page is. Requirement 7 of the 2026-11-14 brief: correlations computed only from officially sourced numbers, with n published beside every value. The definition used here is narrow and applied in code — see §1 — and a relationship that cannot be told apart from zero is published as a null result rather than dropped.

1 · What counts as an officially sourced observation

Both halves of every pair must come from the same game and the same pinned archive row:

  1. the market number is the closing number the archive carries for that game and market (closing_lines.csv), and
  2. the outcome is that game's final score on record (games.csv).

A pair missing either half is dropped, not imputed. The count of surviving pairs is the n on every row. Weeks are counted separately because a slate's games share conditions, so all intervals resample whole weeks, never individual games.

What this does not claim. The archive is a community mirror of the league's own numbers, so the label here is officially sourced values, not read from nfl.com. A correlation computed from nfl.com reads alone would need every game page read; that is what the results feed is for, and the reason the forward test settles only against nfl.com.

3 · How to read a row

  • REPORTED — the interval excludes zero. The sign and size are what the data supports; the causal story is not.
  • NOT DISTINGUISHABLE FROM ZERO — the interval contains zero. This is a real finding: for two-leg parlay pricing it means the legs can be treated as close to independent on this evidence, and no correlation credit can be claimed either way.
  • UNDERPOWERED — fewer than 200 pairs or 20 weeks. The number is withheld, not printed as small.
  • n changes the meaning. A ρ of 0.02 on 5,000 pairs and a ρ of 0.02 on 250 pairs are different statements; that is why the column exists rather than a footnote.
