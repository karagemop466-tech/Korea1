Demotion gate

A pre-registered, fail-closed ladder that returns a live strategy to paper.

Why a demotion gate has to exist before a promotion gate

A promotion rule answers "when has a strategy earned money?". A demotion rule answers "when has it lost the right to be called live?". Most strategy leaderboards have only the first, and the failure mode is predictable: the bar to go live is a single good stretch, and nothing brings the strategy back down when the stretch ends.

Four properties make this gate usable rather than decorative:

  • Pre-registered. Every threshold is a constant dated 2026-11-14T20:15Z, fixed before the gate was ever run against a strategy. Changing one is a new gate and every strategy returns to paper.
  • Fail-closed. Missing evidence never satisfies a condition. A strategy with no authenticated price is not "passing" — it triggers D1.
  • Demotion beats promotion. A strategy meeting every promotion threshold and one demotion trigger is demoted. There is no tie to break.
  • Staking cannot rescue it. Trigger D6 is satisfied by a sign change between a flat stake and a past-only adaptive stake (see signal-staking.html).

What the operator must supply for this gate to ever fire in the other direction

  1. An authenticated price per ticket — book, timestamp, line, price, limit. Without it D1 is permanent and no promotion is possible.
  2. A settlement log that is append-only — a demotion is only meaningful if the tickets leading to it cannot be revised afterwards. The forward ledger already does this for its own picks.
  3. A decision on what a demoted strategy may keep doing — paper-trading continues, but whether it re-enters the same leaderboard or restarts as a new entrant is an owner call. It is written down as a question, with its consequences, on the decision questions page.
